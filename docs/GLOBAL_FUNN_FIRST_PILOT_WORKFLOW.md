# GLOBAL FUNN — First Pilot Production Workflow

## Objective
Produce one complete, source-backed Hindi explainer package from topic selection to a human-approved YouTube upload. Start with one pilot; do not attempt to operate 25 channels at once.

## Pilot format
- Primary format: 4–7 minute Hindi explainer.
- Supporting format: 2–3 Shorts cut from the same verified story.
- Voice: clear, conversational Hindi; explain unfamiliar terms.
- Visuals: original graphics, licensed stock, public-domain material, or assets with documented permission.
- Editorial promise: informative, balanced, transparent about uncertainty.

## Stage 1 — Topic selection
The Trend Researcher proposes 5 candidate topics and records:
- why the topic matters now;
- intended audience and viewer question;
- evidence of interest (search suggestions, reputable coverage, or channel analytics);
- freshness and shelf life;
- risk score for misinformation, legal sensitivity, privacy, or harm.

The orchestrator selects one topic only when there is a clear viewer benefit and enough reliable source material. Avoid topics that depend on rumours, unverified viral clips, or private individuals.

## Stage 2 — Research and fact ledger
The News Researcher creates a fact ledger with:
- claim ID;
- exact factual claim;
- source title, publisher, URL, publication date;
- supporting passage or data point;
- source quality and any disagreement;
- confidence: confirmed / qualified / unverified.

Prefer primary documents, official datasets, direct statements, and multiple independent reputable sources. Record what is not known. Do not turn allegations into facts.

## Stage 3 — Script and fact-check
The Hindi Scriptwriter drafts:
1. a factual opening hook;
2. context and timeline;
3. the key explanation;
4. what is confirmed vs. uncertain;
5. practical viewer takeaway;
6. source note and closing.

The Fact-check & Compliance agent checks every material claim against the fact ledger, flags misleading framing, privacy concerns, defamation risk, copyright issues, and missing disclosures. Any unresolved high-risk claim means HOLD and revise.

## Stage 4 — Production package
The Shorts Producer and Visual Brand Director prepare:
- scene-by-scene visual plan;
- original graphic and B-roll checklist;
- voice-over script with pronunciation notes;
- captions/subtitles;
- thumbnail concept with no fabricated imagery or deceptive claims;
- music/SFX asset log showing licence or permission.

Do not clone a real person's voice or use an identifiable person's likeness without appropriate permission. Do not use copyrighted music or footage unless licensed or otherwise lawfully usable.

## Stage 5 — YouTube packaging
The YouTube SEO Strategist prepares:
- 3 accurate title options;
- concise description with source links;
- relevant search phrases and tags (tags are secondary to title, description and viewer satisfaction);
- chapters for long-form video;
- thumbnail text and accessibility alt description;
- pinned comment with a useful question and source/correction route.

No keyword stuffing, misleading thumbnails, fake urgency, or guaranteed performance claims.

## Stage 6 — Pre-publication QA
The Analytics Insights agent defines the measurement plan before release:
- impressions and click-through rate;
- first 30-second retention;
- average view duration and percentage viewed;
- Shorts viewed vs. swiped away;
- returning viewers and meaningful comments.

The Orchestrator verifies all deliverables and creates a release checklist. A human must approve the final rendered video, title, thumbnail, description, visibility and schedule.

## Stage 7 — Publish and learn
Only after explicit human approval:
- upload through the channel owner's authorized YouTube workflow;
- check title, thumbnail, subtitles, links and visibility in preview;
- publish or schedule;
- monitor early comments and correct factual errors transparently;
- review performance after 24 hours and 7 days.

Never publish automatically from an unreviewed draft. Never spend money on promotion or contact sponsors without separate approval.

## Required handoff record
Each pilot must retain:
- topic and audience rationale;
- source/fact ledger;
- script version and fact-check status;
- asset and licence log;
- final video and thumbnail review status;
- SEO package;
- human approver and approval timestamp;
- publish URL and post-publication metrics.

## Definition of done
The pilot is complete only when the source-backed script, final reviewed video, rights log, SEO package and approval record exist. A draft or generated voice-over alone is not a published video.
