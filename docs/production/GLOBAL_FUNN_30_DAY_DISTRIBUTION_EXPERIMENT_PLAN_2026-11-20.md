# GLOBAL FUNN — 30-Day Distribution Experiment Plan
**Prepared:** 20 November 2026  
**Status:** Operating plan; experiments begin only when the first video is actually published.

## Objective
Build a repeatable, low-cost discovery loop for GLOBAL FUNN. Optimize for returning viewers and meaningful watch time, not vanity views or guaranteed revenue.

## Guardrails
- Do not publish until the owner approves the final video, title, thumbnail, description, and visibility.
- Do not buy views, subscribers, comments, or engagement.
- Do not repost copyrighted clips or use unlicensed music, footage, logos, or screenshots.
- Clearly label AI-generated or synthetic media where required; verify factual claims and dates before release.
- Keep one variable change per experiment where possible. Record the change and date.
- No paid promotion or subscription spend without explicit budget approval.
- If no video is published, record the experiment as **not started**, not as a failed result.

## Four-week operating sequence
### Week 1 — First release and baseline
1. Publish Pilot 02 only after final factual, audio, caption, thumbnail, and owner-approval checks.
2. Capture baseline at 24 hours and 7 days: impressions, click-through rate, views, average view duration, average percentage viewed, returning viewers, subscribers gained, and traffic sources.
3. Publish the two companion Shorts only after checking that each works independently and points viewers to the long-form topic where appropriate.
4. Record the exact title, thumbnail version, opening hook, publication time, and links.

### Week 2 — Packaging experiment
1. Review impressions and click-through rate alongside traffic source and audience size; do not compare unlike traffic sources as if they were identical.
2. If impressions are sufficient to interpret, test one revised title or thumbnail concept, not both at once.
3. Keep a dated change log and compare a similar time window.
4. Do not claim a winner from tiny samples; label the result **inconclusive** when evidence is weak.

### Week 3 — Retention experiment
1. Inspect the audience-retention curve and identify the largest early drop-off and one later drop-off.
2. For the next upload, test a shorter opening that states the viewer benefit and evidence early.
3. Remove repeated introductions and unnecessary channel branding from the opening.
4. Compare average view duration and percentage viewed with videos of similar format and length.

### Week 4 — Topic and format decision
1. Compare the long-form video and Shorts separately.
2. Identify which audience question, hook, and format produced the strongest qualified response.
3. Select one follow-up topic based on evidence, editorial relevance, and ability to verify facts.
4. Decide: repeat, revise, or stop the format. Do not infer revenue from views.

## Weekly measurement sheet
| Metric | Record | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions | YouTube Studio value + date | Distribution opportunity |
| CTR | Value + traffic source | Packaging signal; context matters |
| Views | Value + date window | Reach, not earnings |
| Average view duration | Time | Depth of viewing |
| Average percentage viewed | Percentage | Retention relative to length |
| First 30-second retention | Percentage/curve note | Opening effectiveness |
| Returning viewers | Value + date window | Audience habit |
| Subscribers gained | Value + date window | Conversion signal |
| Shorts viewed vs swiped away | Value | Short-form hook signal |
| Comments/questions | Themes, anonymized | Audience needs and next topics |

## Decision rules
- **Repeat:** strong evidence across more than one relevant metric and no material factual/safety issue.
- **Revise:** reach exists but packaging or retention shows a specific weakness.
- **Hold:** too little data, inconsistent traffic mix, or no published asset yet.
- **Stop:** repeated weak response after controlled revisions, or a factual/safety problem that cannot be corrected.

## No-cost distribution checklist
- Use YouTube Studio's native title, description, thumbnail, chapters, subtitles, end screen, and playlist features where available.
- Share the public link only in relevant communities that permit self-promotion; add a useful explanation rather than spam.
- Create a concise community post from the video's key takeaway when the channel feature is available.
- Repurpose one factual insight into a text post only after checking it against the source ledger.
- Keep a simple link and publication log. Never imply that an asset was posted when it was only drafted.
