## 2. Record with an Android phone — no paid AI voice required
1. Choose a quiet, soft-furnished room. Turn off fan/TV if practical; silence notifications and incoming-call interruptions.
2. Use the phone's built-in voice recorder. If available, choose the highest-quality standard setting (WAV or high-quality M4A/AAC); avoid voice effects, noise filters that distort speech, and automatic music.
3. Keep the phone about 15–20 cm from the mouth, slightly off-axis. Do not hold or rub the microphone.
4. Record a 10-second test. Listen with headphones: speech should be clear, not clipped, boomy, or drowned by room noise.
5. Record the long-form narration in separate files by script section. Say the section heading only if it is meant to be spoken; otherwise leave a 2-second pause before the first line. Leave 2 seconds at the end of each section.
6. If a line is stumbled, pause, repeat the complete sentence, then continue. Do not stop the recording for every small mistake.
7. Record each Short as its own separate file after the long-form narration.
8. Keep the original untouched recordings. Do not add background music, effects, or aggressive noise reduction at this stage.

## 4. Delivery and quality check
Before upload, confirm:
- Every script section has a corresponding recording.
- The beginning and ending of each file are not cut off.
- Voice volume is consistent; no clipping, loud handling noise, or private information is audible.
- Names, numbers, dates, helpline details and reporting routes are read exactly as written in the fact-checked script.
- No unsupported claim is added while improvising.
- The two Shorts are separate files and are not merely clipped from an unreviewed long take.

If a number, legal detail, date or helpline line is unclear, pause and check the source ledger before recording again. For financial cyber fraud, preserve the script's instruction to use the official 1930 route / National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal; do not improvise alternative contact numbers.
