# GLOBAL FUNN — 7-Day Pilot Production Sprint

Sprint start: 20 November 2026
Status: proposed operating cadence; no content is approved or published by this document.

## Strategic guardrails
- Run only the three pilots: GLOBAL FUNN News, Jharkhand Ground Report, and AI Tools Hindi Lab.
- Build a repeatable production loop before opening more channels.
- Prefer vertical-first Shorts, clear subtitles and authentic explanation; adapt format to audience evidence rather than copying unrelated viral videos.
- A trend chart is a discovery signal, not a guarantee of views. The India chart snapshot on 20 November 2026 showed Entertainment and Music dominating the list; News & Politics had 6 of 49 entries, while Education and Science & Technology had 2 each. Treat this as a snapshot, not a permanent category ranking.
- No auto-publishing. A human approves the final export and all metadata.

If the team cannot meet quality gates, reduce volume rather than skipping checks.

## Day-by-day workflow

### Day 1 — Discover and select
- Collect 10 topic candidates across the three pilots.
- Score audience relevance, evidence quality, originality, production effort and repeatability.
- Select one long-video topic per pilot and two Shorts angles per topic.
- Save sources, publication dates and claims to verify.

### Day 2 — Research and outline
- Open primary sources; confirm dates, names, figures and context.
- Draft a one-page fact pack and claim ledger.
- Prepare a 5–8 minute long-video outline and two 30–45 second Short outlines per channel.
- Hold any topic whose central claim cannot be verified.

### Day 3 — Script and visual plan
- Write spoken Hindi scripts with a clear hook, explanation, practical takeaway and source note.
- Create shot lists, screen-recording steps, graphic prompts and subtitle text.
- Confirm that footage, music, images and fonts have appropriate usage rights.

### Day 4 — Produce
- Record voice-over or presenter audio.
- Capture real product screens for AI tutorials; do not simulate successful functionality.
- Build simple graphics and edit vertical-first Shorts plus the long-form cut.
- Check audio intelligibility and subtitle accuracy.

### Day 5 — Independent quality review
- Fact-check every material claim against the saved sources.
- Check defamation, privacy, financial/medical/legal advice risks, misleading synthetic media and copyright.
- Confirm title/thumbnail accurately represent the video.
- Mark each asset PASS, REVISE or HOLD.

### Day 6 — Human approval and publish
- Human owner reviews the final rendered files, titles, thumbnails, descriptions and visibility settings.
- Upload only approved assets.
- Record the live URL, publication time and final metadata.
- If no human approval or platform access is available, keep the video as a draft; do not claim publication.

### Day 7 — Measure and learn
- Capture actual impressions, click-through rate, views, average view duration, retention, subscribers gained and meaningful comments where available.
- Compare each video with the channel's own baseline; do not compare raw views across unrelated channels.
- Choose one packaging experiment and one content experiment for the next sprint.

## Metrics and decision rules
- Do not set universal CTR or retention targets before collecting channel baselines.
- Compare like-for-like format, topic and traffic source.
- If impressions are low, investigate topic demand and distribution before changing thumbnails repeatedly.
- If impressions are healthy but clicks are weak, test title/thumbnail clarity.
- If clicks are healthy but early retention is weak, revise the hook and expectation match.
- If retention is healthy but subscribers are weak, improve channel promise and next-video pathway.
- Do not infer revenue from views; use actual YouTube Studio monetisation data.
